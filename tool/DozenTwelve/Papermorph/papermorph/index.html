What it does

Papermorph is an agent skill that turns textbook PDFs, stories, jokes or books into narrated, interactive web lessons, paper-puppet picture-book films, documentaries and upright shorts. It plans the work as chapters or scenes, then a script, then a storyboard. It voices the narration with word-level timing, animates the picture to those times, and checks its own output. Each chapter or episode can also be exported to a 1080p MP4 with an .srt file.

Where it fits your pipeline

It is an alternative to the Kling-based path rather than a step that follows it. It covers story to storyboard, voiced narration, animation, music, SFX and captions in one pass. It could slot in after Idea -> story, or after Story -> episodes. Its output is paper-puppet or illustrated film, not Kling clips, so the look differs from your reference-image workflow. It improves pacing by timing the picture to word-level narration. It also checks its own output with contact sheets and blank-frame checks, which may cut some manual review. It does not do photoreal character video or hook and cliffhanger writing specifically, and the README does not say it does. Optional paid steps can make clips from a still, but whether they fit your Kling references is unclear.

Install

  • npx skills add DozenTwelve/Papermorph --skill papermorph --agent claude-code

Then copy the skill's folder into ~/.claude/skills/.

How to use

/papermorph Turn my short story in story.md into a paper-puppet film with acted voices, subtitles, sound effects and music. Suggest a first episode to try and recommend options at each step.

The skill reads your story and says what kind of book it is. It suggests a first episode and how much animation it needs, then offers a recommended option at each step. Your choices are saved in style.json so later episodes match. You get a static web page film with voice, captions, SFX and music. It can be rendered to a 1080p MP4 plus .srt with scripts/export.py. You need uv, ffmpeg and Playwright browsers installed.
